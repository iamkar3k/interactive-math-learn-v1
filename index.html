Lesson 1 · Trigonometry

Where sine waves come from

A point moving around a circle of radius 1 has a height and a horizontal distance from the center. Those two numbers are sin θ and cos θ. If you plot the height over time, you get a sine wave.

  • Drag the point around the circle to set θ yourself.
  • Pause at θ = 90°. Why is sin θ exactly 1 there?
  • Check that sin²θ + cos²θ stays 1 at every angle.
sin²θ + cos²θ = 1

Lesson 2 · Calculus

Area under a curve, one rectangle at a time

Curved shapes are hard to measure, but rectangles are easy. Fill the area under a curve with thin rectangles and add up their areas. This is a Riemann sum. As you add more rectangles the error shrinks toward zero, and the limit is the integral.

  • Press play and watch the error shrink as n grows.
  • Compare left, midpoint and right sums. Which one gets close fastest?
  • Pick the cubic: rectangles below the x-axis count as negative area.

Lesson 3 · Geometry

Proving Pythagoras without algebra

Take four copies of the same right triangle and put them inside a square with side a + b. In one arrangement the empty space is a single tilted square, c². Slide the triangles around and the empty space becomes two squares, a² and b². Nothing was added or taken away, so the two empty areas must be equal.

  • Change a and b. The proof works for every right triangle.
  • Use the scrubber to stop partway through the rearrangement.
  • Try a = 3, b = 4. What is c?
a² + b² = c²
